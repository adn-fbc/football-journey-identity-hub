# Player Development Competency Framework

## 1. Purpose

The competency framework is used to measure growth across a player’s development journey. It is intended to support evidence-based assessment, not to reduce a player to a single rating.

The framework includes:

- Technical competencies
- Tactical competencies
- Physical competencies
- Psychological competencies
- Social competencies

## 2. Assessment principles

1. Competencies are assessed over time, not in a single moment.
2. Each competency needs evidence before a formal rating is accepted.
3. Age and developmental stage affect expectations.
4. Ratings must be interpreted alongside context, not in isolation.
5. Development recommendations should explain how to improve.

## 3. Technical competencies

| Competency | Description | Assessment criteria | Development indicators | Evidence requirements | Assessment method | Age considerations |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ball control | Ability to receive and manipulate the ball under pressure | clean first touch, body shape, control in motion | improved first touch and balance | session observations, match footage, technical drills | coach observation + short drill score | U8 to U10 focus on first touch and confidence |
| Passing accuracy | Ability to pass with control and precision | weight and direction of pass, use of both feet | more consistent passing under pressure | training drill logs, match passes | observation and checklist | age-specific passing distance and technique |
| Dribbling | Ability to move with the ball at speed | change of direction, close control, posture | better decision-making and control at speed | match sequences and small-sided play | coach scoring rubric | U8 uses simple dribble and escape patterns |
| Shooting | Ability to finish effectively | shot technique, composure, decision-making | better placement and accuracy | match goals, trials, set-piece analysis | coach and match review | emphasize technique before power |
| Receiving under pressure | Ability to control the ball in contact situations | first touch after challenge, body orientation | better controlled touches in crowded situations | session and match analysis | drill plus observation | focus on confidence and safe touches |

## 4. Tactical competencies

| Competency | Description | Assessment criteria | Development indicators | Evidence requirements | Assessment method | Age considerations |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Decision making | Ability to choose the right action in context | choices under pressure, awareness, timing | better passing, support angles, risks taken | match video, coach notes, small-sided games | match-based observation | younger players focus on simple choices |
| Positioning | Ability to hold effective space and shape | field awareness, balance, support | more intelligent spacing and support play | match scenarios | observation + tactical analysis | emphasize role understanding in age groups |
| Game awareness | Ability to read situations and anticipate play | scanning, awareness of teammate and opposition | improved scanning and support timing | coach observation and match notes | match review | teach simple tactical cues first |
| Team play | Ability to support group structure | passing combinations, pressing, recovery | smoother combinations and support movements | match and training sessions | structured observation | emphasize simple patterns and roles |
| Transition play | Ability to attack and defend in transitions | reaction speed and decision-making in turnover moments | faster compactness and recognition | drills and match review | observation and scorecard | focus on quick transitions and responsibilities |

## 5. Physical competencies

| Competency | Description | Assessment criteria | Development indicators | Evidence requirements | Assessment method | Age considerations |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Speed | Ability to accelerate and change pace | acceleration, reaction time, first step | quicker execution and recovery | sprint drills, match moments | timed test + observation | age thresholds to avoid overemphasis |
| Agility | Ability to change direction efficiently | balance, coordination, footwork | improved body control | shuttle runs and agility drills | drill test + coach view | emphasize coordination in early ages |
| Endurance | Ability to sustain effort over time | repeat sprint performance, stamina | more resilience in full sessions and matches | observations and match minutes | coach + match-based review | avoid excessive load in minors |
| Strength | Ability to hold position and manage contact | body control, balance, resistance | more stable play when challenged | observation and physical tasks | coach and session review | focus on coordination before maximal strength |
| Coordination | Ability to control body movement in sport-specific tasks | rhythm, control, balance | smoother movement under pressure | training drills and observation | practical task review | key for early development |

## 6. Psychological competencies

| Competency | Description | Assessment criteria | Development indicators | Evidence requirements | Assessment method | Age considerations |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Confidence | Ability to act without fear of failure | willingness to take initiative | more willingness to receive the ball and shoot | observation and self-reflection | coach review and player self-rating | support confidence-building in younger players |
| Resilience | Ability to recover from mistakes and setbacks | response after errors, focus, bounce-back | better recovery after pressure or mistakes | match review, coach notes | observation and reflective check-in | emphasize safe recovery and emotional regulation |
| Concentration | Ability to stay engaged in play | focus, decision-making, discipline | stronger attention in sessions and matches | observations, match context | coach + player input | age-specific attention span considerations |
| Motivation | Ability to stay engaged and driven | effort, desire to improve, persistence | sustained participation and effort | coach observation and goal progress | observation + self-report | avoid overloading younger players |
| Emotional regulation | Ability to manage stress and emotions | composure after setbacks | calmer reaction to game events | observation, coach conversation | reflective discussion | essential for younger players and pressure environments |

## 7. Social competencies

| Competency | Description | Assessment criteria | Development indicators | Evidence requirements | Assessment method | Age considerations |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Communication | Ability to talk and support teammates | clear instructions, listening, team talk | improved usage of simple team cues | sessions and match communication | coach observation | teach clear, age-appropriate communication |
| Teamwork | Ability to contribute to group play | support, encouragement, role fulfilment | better collective play and support | match review and training | coach observation | early age focus on cooperation |
| Respect | Ability to act respectfully within the sport context | fairness, discipline, respect for rules | improved self-control and role fulfilment | coach observation and conduct record | structured checklist | essential for all age groups |
| Leadership | Ability to lead peers and improve team play | initiative, encouragement, responsibility | increased peer influence in training | coach notes and session tasks | observation and role assignment | age-appropriate scaffolding only |
| Responsibility | Ability to fulfill role and commitments | attendance, punctuality, task completion | stronger reliability and habits | attendance and coach observation | checklist and trend review | builds long-term player maturity |

## 8. Recommended assessment methods

### Observation-based assessment
Used for:
- technical and tactical development
- social and psychological indicators
- routine player review

### Drill-based assessment
Used for:
- ball control
- passing accuracy
- agility and speed
- reaction and coordination

### Match-based assessment
Used for:
- decision making
- positioning
- game awareness
- performance under pressure

### Self-assessment and coach review
Used for:
- confidence
- motivation
- emotional regulation
- responsibility

### Formal assessment cycle
Used for:
- periodic longitudinal scorekeeping
- snapshot comparison across a season
- progress review for planning and goal setting

## 9. Competency evidence requirements

A competency rating should only be considered valid when supported by at least one of the following:

- training observation
- match evidence
- formal assessment score
- goal progress linked to the competency
- documented session or context notes

## 10. Historical progress tracking

The system must retain:

- baseline scores
- recalculated scores across cycles
- revision history on ratings
- date and assessor context
- source evidence references

This ensures that progress is tracked as a longitudinal story and not as a one-off score.

## 11. Development recommendation model

For each competency, the product should support a recommendation such as:

- focus on body orientation under pressure
- build first-touch comfort in receiving under pressure
- improve passing technique through short, repeated patterns
- encourage more scanning and support play without the ball
- strengthen resilience after recoveries or mistakes

Recommendations should be attached to the evidence and not treated as a replacement for the official record.

## 12. Final framework principle

A single rating cannot define a player. The system should present a player as a profile of competencies, evidence, context, and growth over time.
