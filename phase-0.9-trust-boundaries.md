# Phase 0.9 — Trust Boundaries and Data Access Concepts

**Status:** Conceptual security review only. These are access principles and threat boundaries, not a role matrix approval, authorization specification, RLS policy, API design, or implementation instruction. Authentication, identity verification, guardian relationship verification, guardian authority, consent, and authorization are distinct evaluations.

## Trust boundary principles

1. A provider-authenticated session can establish control of a login identity only. It does not by itself resolve a Person, Player, Organization Membership, guardian relationship, grantor authority, or resource permission.
2. Person identity and Player identity require their own explicit resolution policy. Knowing a National Football ID is not a secret, authentication factor, or authorization proof.
3. A claimed guardian relationship, verified relationship, guardian authority, and scoped consent are different concepts. Each depends on separate evidence and policy; no one concept automatically implies another.
4. Organization Membership, Organization Owner status, staff assignment, organizer assignment, and Platform Admin status are not interchangeable. Authority must be limited to the approved subject, resource, action, purpose, and scope.
5. A new affiliation does not grant historical access to another organization's records. A Player Journey projection is not a permission grant.
6. Consent is a policy input. It cannot replace authenticated identity, active assignment, source ownership, classification rules, or a stronger prohibition.
7. Client UI role, localStorage, mock actor, route visibility, and client-supplied IDs are untrusted selectors, not security boundaries. Future authorization must be enforced at trusted server-side boundaries, with data-layer enforcement designed separately.
8. Tenant isolation is a conceptual requirement wherever organization-scoped information is introduced; organization hierarchy must not silently inherit access.
9. Platform Admin requires least privilege, defined operational purpose, restricted elevation, and audit; no blanket business-data access is implied.
10. Audit records should capture the minimum necessary security/accountability facts. They do not authorize access and must not become a copy of business records. Append-only does not mean indefinite retention; retention and deletion remain policy decisions.

## Actor access concepts

| Actor | Purpose of access | Information that may be needed | Boundary that must not be crossed | Identity / authority / consent dependency | Risk of overly broad access |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Anonymous visitor | Browse information explicitly published for public use. | Approved public organization or competition projections only. | No person-private, player-private, child-sensitive, guardian/legal, roster, or non-public match information. | No authenticated identity; publication and classification policy must explicitly permit the projection. | Enumeration, child discovery, re-identification, or exposure through a public lookup. |
| Authenticated account | Use account functions and request operations as a verified session subject. | Own account settings and only resources authorized after identity resolution. | Session control does not grant Person, Player, organization, guardian, or platform scope. | Provider session verification and account lifecycle; P-01–P-05/P-11 unresolved. | Account linking/recovery abuse or treating authentication as authorization. |
| Person identity | Maintain or resolve a human identity across roles. | Minimum identity attributes and restricted verification/resolution evidence. | No automatic Player identity, account link, role grant, or access to another person's history. | Approved identity resolution and verified evidence; D-33 and P-05/P-11. | False merges/splits, identity takeover, or unnecessary collection of identity evidence. |
| Player identity | Maintain the player's stable football identity and permitted personal context. | Own identity, approved affiliations, and projections allowed by policy. | No organization ownership; no ID reassignment; no access inferred solely from knowing the ID. | Account-person-player resolution and applicable self-access policy; D-24/D-33. | ID enumeration/disclosure or incorrect identity merge/correction. |
| Organization member | Perform current organization-scoped operational work. | Minimum organization, team, roster, schedule, or competition information needed for assigned duties. | No automatic access to other tenants, full player history, guardian evidence, unrelated sensitive records, or Platform Admin functions. | Verified membership, current exact-scope assignment, action and classification policy; P-06/P-07/D-26. | Cross-organization exposure, stale access, or privilege inheritance. |
| Organization Owner | Administer the organization within verified delegated authority. | Organization governance and necessary current operational information. | Not Platform Admin; no automatic global player search, former-organization history, NFI change, consent override, or unnecessary personal data. | Verified organization control and explicit scoped delegation; P-06/P-07/D-25/D-26. | Tenant-wide overreach, ownership transfer abuse, or consent bypass. |
| Coach / development staff | Deliver assigned training and create/review authorized development records. | Current assigned roster, schedules, attendance, and permitted observations/feedback. | No unassigned player/team, guardian/legal evidence, unrelated organization history, or unrestricted raw observation access. | Current assignment, author/reviewer separation where ADR-09 applies, classification, and resolved P-16 visibility. | Child-sensitive observation exposure, self-verification, or access persisting after assignment ends. |
| Guardian | Manage permitted child-facing functions for a specific player. | Only child projections and actions that policy allows for this relationship and grant. | Relationship verification alone is not authority or consent; no other guardian's grants, unrelated children, or prohibited sensitive records. | Relationship verification, separately established authority, current scoped consent, and policy; P-12–P-15. | False guardian claims, family conflict, excessive surveillance, or scope escalation. |
| Authorized Grantor | Issue a scoped grant only for a subject/action the grantor is authorized to approve. | Authority evidence and grant details necessary for the decision. | Authority is not global; no grant based only on guardian label, org role, or UI toggle; cannot override prohibited classification. | Subject-specific authority, purpose, scope, recipient, time, and policy; P-12–P-15/P-17. | Unauthorized sharing, consent laundering, or one guardian affecting another's authority. |
| Competition organizer | Operate an assigned competition/event workflow. | Minimum team, registration, roster, schedule, and match data needed for that operation. | No general player directory, unrelated development history, guardian/legal data, or cross-competition access. | Verified organizer identity, exact assignment, data minimization, eligibility/registration rules; D-28/D-34/D-36. | Unauthorized participant discovery, roster manipulation, or overcollection. |
| Platform Admin | Maintain platform operations and respond to approved incidents. | Minimum operational metadata; exceptional business-data access only under an approved, recorded procedure. | No routine player/guardian browsing, consent bypass, silent impersonation, or unrestricted export. | Strongly controlled operational identity, explicit purpose, bounded elevation, and audit; P-06/D-32. | Systemic insider exposure, unaccountable support access, or audit bypass. |
| System/service actor | Perform an explicitly authorized internal processing task. | Narrow inputs and outputs required for one approved task. | No implicit superuser privilege, unrestricted cross-tenant access, or use of a service credential as user authority. | Owner-approved workload identity, task scope, least privilege, and traceable actor context; provider/runtime remain unresolved. | Automated mass disclosure, confused-deputy behavior, or untraceable mutation. |

## Threat considerations

| Threat | Conceptual boundary / concern | Decisions or controls required before implementation |
|---|---|---|
| ID enumeration | Predictable or exposed identifiers can enable profile discovery. | D-24 issuance/lookup projection; P-17/P-19 search policy; rate and disclosure policy. |
| ID disclosure | Football ID may be visible in screens, URLs, exports, or logs. | Treat ID as non-secret but minimize unnecessary exposure; approve public lookup fields (D-24/P-17). |
| Impersonation | Client-selected role/identity or weak account recovery may spoof a subject. | P-05/P-11 identity linking/recovery; server-verified session and actor derivation. |
| Duplicate ID issuance | Collisions or duplicate Player records may break identity continuity. | D-24 issuance/collision rules and D-33 duplicate/merge/split process. |
| Identity correction abuse | Unauthorized merge, split, or correction can redirect history. | D-33 review authority, rationale, provenance, and preserved historical references. |
| Cross-organization exposure | Membership, hierarchy, or new affiliation may be treated as inherited access. | P-06/P-07/P-17; exact-scope checks and explicit non-inheritance rules. |
| Unauthorized profile discovery | Search/index results can disclose child existence before detail access. | P-19 search fields/recipients/revocation; child discovery remains disabled under DECISION-002. |
| Guardian authority conflict | Multiple guardians may assert conflicting authority or revocation. | P-12/P-13 authority evidence, conflict precedence, and safeguarding escalation. |
| Consent scope escalation | A broad toggle or stale grant may authorize more than intended. | P-14/P-15 exact subject/category/action/purpose/recipient/time and revocation behavior. |
| Unauthorized observation access | Raw coaching notes may be exposed by assignment or a general consent toggle. | P-16 visibility decision; distinguish raw observation from reviewed feedback and classification. |
| Audit over-collection | Logs can replicate sensitive business payloads or become a bypass. | D-32/P-18 minimum event content, access, integrity, retention, and deletion policy. |
| Media leakage | Real-child photos/media may leak through asset URLs, screenshots, demo accounts, or exports. | DECISION-002; verify media provenance and disable real-child media workflows until gates pass. |

## Child-safety and minimization boundary

Until guardian verification, guardian authority, scoped consent, child-safety controls, safeguarding, auditability, and independent review are designed and tested:

- **Permitted during initial development/testing:** adult authentication test accounts only; synthetic/dummy player data only.
- **Prohibited during initial development/testing:** real child personal data; real child photos, videos, or other identifiable media; real-world child identity verification experiments; real-child scouting; real-child player discovery/searching; and real-child profile sharing or distribution.
- These restrictions remain in force until guardian verification, guardian authority, scoped consent, safeguarding, auditability, independent review, and release approval are designed, tested, and formally approved under DECISION-002. A prototype toggle, local UI state, mock actor, or local consent panel does not satisfy these requirements.
- Organization membership or ownership does not automatically grant global access to player information or cross-organization history.

Potential leakage paths include fixtures, screenshots, seed data, demo accounts, logs, analytics, exports, test reports, media URLs, and error messages. Repository checks found fictional-labelled mock data, aggregate fictional analytics, local player image assets, and error reporting that can include messages/stacks. In the prototype consent panel, photo, statistics, and journey controls default on; the UI copy says permissions are off unless enabled. The controls update local component state and show a toast only; they do not create persisted authorization evidence. This describes prototype behavior and is not an approved consent policy. The two local player JPEG assets were not opened; their authenticity, provenance, licensing, and suitability for external demonstration remain **NOT VERIFIED**. Screenshots outside indexed repository paths, runtime logs, telemetry destinations, external demo accounts, and exported files were also **NOT VERIFIED**. Do not infer those channels are safe, synthetic, licensed, or free of personal data from the mock-data comment alone.

## Implementation boundary

This document does not select authentication, a provider, server framework, policy engine, RLS structure, or audit mechanism. When implementation is separately authorized, trusted server-side authorization must distinguish authentication, identity resolution, membership/assignment, guardian verification, authority, consent, classification, source ownership, and requested action. Every unknown or conflicting input should be treated as unresolved rather than granted authority.
